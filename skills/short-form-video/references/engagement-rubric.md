# Engagement rubric

Used **twice**: on the script before spending anything, and on the finished
render. Same criteria, different evidence.

Score each 0–3. **Anything at 0 or 1 must be fixed before shipping.**

## Pre-render (text only, free)

| # | Criterion | 3 = |
|---|-----------|-----|
| 1 | **Hook** | First sentence makes the next 3s feel necessary; works on this topic only |
| 2 | **One idea** | A single teachable claim, not a category survey |
| 3 | **Concreteness** | Every beat is filmable; numbers and actions, not abstractions |
| 4 | **Pacing** | 30–60s at 165 wpm; sentence lengths vary |
| 5 | **Landing** | Ends on one memorable action, not a summary |
| 6 | **Accuracy** | Every factual claim is correct and unfabricated |

## Post-render (view actual frames)

Sample frames across the video and **look at them**. Then:

| # | Criterion | 3 = | Fix |
|---|-----------|-----|-----|
| 7 | **First 2 seconds** | A frame worth stopping on; avatar or motion present immediately | script + footage |
| 8 | **Visual/narration match** | The frame at time *t* shows what is being said at *t* | footage (free) |
| 9 | **Caption legibility** | Readable against every background it crosses | footage or style (free) |
| 10 | **Cut rhythm** | No shot over ~4s; cuts land on phrase boundaries | footage (free) |
| 11 | **Dead air** | No silence over 0.6s that is not doing dramatic work | script (costs credits) |
| 12 | **Avatar staging** | Presenter never covers the subject of the shot or the captions | free |
| 13 | **On-topic footage** | Zero clips that are merely "adjacent" to the subject | footage (free) |

## Repair order

Fix in this order — it is cheapest first, and visual fixes often dissolve what
looked like script problems:

1. **Re-pick footage** → re-run `build`. Free.
2. **Adjust caption position or avatar staging** → re-run `build`. Free.
3. **Rewrite the script** → re-run `narrate` and `avatar`. **Costs credits —
   tell the user first.**

## The honest test

After the rubric, watch it once as a viewer, not an author. Ask: *would I still
be watching at second fifteen?*

The rubric is a tool for finding problems, not a substitute for that judgement.
A video can score well and still be dull, and that is the only failure the
brief actually cares about.
